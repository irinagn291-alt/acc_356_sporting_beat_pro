# Sporting Beat

## What it is

Sporting Beat is a timing game about making sound effects for short animated sports clips. Players tap, swipe, and hold virtual props to match the action, then review, edit, and save their soundtracks. It is for players who enjoy rhythm challenges or want to make their own version of a sports soundtrack.

## Launch and onboarding

1. On first launch, the app opens directly to “Foley desk.” The header shows “Sporting Beat,” “FOLEY STUDIO,” and “0 / 16 done.” The monitor starts looping the eight-second “Into the net” clip under “PICTURE ONLY.”
2. “Prop table” shows “Tap a prop to try it.” The first props are “Pillow” with “Tap,” “Keys” with “Swipe / tap,” and “Corrugated cardboard” with “Swipe / tap.” In compact layouts, the last tile says “Cardboard.” Try the props immediately to hear their sounds.
3. Tap “Land 2 of 3 cues” to open the optional brief. It shows “Director’s orders,” “Into the net,” “Land 2 of 3 cues,” “±350 ms,” “Pillow at the kick. Keys at the net. Cardboard when the crowd stands.” and “Follow the coloured cues on the monitor. The prop dots match the three tracks.” Tap “OK” to close it.
4. Tap “Record.” The monitor switches to “RECORDING,” the table says “Live props,” and “STAND BY” appears with “3,” “2,” and “1.” After the countdown, play the props along with the clip. The take ends automatically and opens “Screening.” “Cancel” stops the attempt.
5. “Creative take” is available before recording. Turning it on shows “Make your own soundtrack. Save it to the shelf at any score.”

There is no separate welcome screen or required tutorial. Once an order or take has been saved as part of the studio’s progress, a cold launch opens “Orders” with the last opened or saved order loaded.

## Screens

The persistent tabs are “Orders,” “Foley desk,” “Screening,” and “Tape shelf.” The header shows the campaign count, such as “0 / 16 done.” The control names and order catalogue below use US English, with UK English differences listed under Platform.

When all orders are approved, the card gives way to “All sixteen orders approved.”

### “Foley desk”

The monitor is headed by the current clip title and shows the animation, time, progress, and colour-coded cue guides. Its status is “PICTURE ONLY” during rehearsal and “RECORDING” during countdown and capture. The prop dots correspond to “Contact,” “Movement,” and “Ambience.”

| Gesture label | Prop labels and action |
|---|---|
| “Tap” | “Pillow,” “Cloth pouch,” “Wood blocks,” “Brass bowl,” and “Tin lid” make a short sound on touch. |
| “Swipe / tap” | “Keys,” “Corrugated cardboard” or compact “Cardboard,” and “Ratchet” make a short sound on touch. Swiping varies its intensity. |
| “Hold / drag” | “Brush” and “Stretched rubber” sustain sound until released. Drag while holding to vary intensity. |

Each clip offers its own subset of props. The props animate as they are played and give touch feedback on supported hardware.

- “Record” starts a three-second countdown and records the player’s prop actions for the length of the clip. “Cancel” stops and discards that attempt. Completing the recording opens “Screening.”
- The “Land 2 of 3 cues” button, or the corresponding target for the current order, opens the brief with the cue window, the clip’s instructions, and any special conditions. “OK” closes it.
- “Creative take” toggles a free soundtrack mode and hides the rehearsal and recording cue guides. The take can still be scored, played, edited, and saved.
- “Timeline” opens the editor when a take exists.

### “Screening”

The current clip appears under “PLAYBACK MONITOR.” A recorded take shows its cue total, such as “2 / 3 cues,” and one result heading. “Precision cut” means every cue was precise and the special conditions were met. “Order approved” means the cue target and applicable conditions were met. “Keep working” means the take fell short. “Creative take” identifies a free soundtrack. A missed special condition shows “Review the special condition on the order.”

Each cue row shows its name, target time, and feedback. Feedback labels are “Precise,” “On cue,” “Too early,” “Too late,” “Wrong sound,” “No action,” “Action used,” “Released early,” and “Released late.” “Action used” means the same prop action has already been credited to another cue.

Cue names used across the catalogue are “Kick,” “Net touch,” “Crowd rises,” “Pass,” “One-touch shot,” “Post strike,” “Rebound,” “Crowd gasp,” “Rain bed,” “Serve,” “Bounce,” “Return,” “Slide,” “Second bounce,” “Second return,” “Third bounce,” “Third return,” “Drop shot,” “Shoes on court,” “First dribble,” “Second dribble,” “Third dribble,” “Fourth dribble,” “Catch,” “Shot release,” “Rim strike,” “Starting gate,” “Slipstream,” “Track rush,” “Pedal click,” “Bell,” “Second pedal click,” and “Second cheer.”

- “Play this take” starts the current take from the beginning and changes to “Stop” during playback. “Play first take” appears when a separate first take is available for comparison.
- “Full screen” opens full-screen playback and starts the current take.
- “Timeline” opens the editor for the current take.
- “Save take” adds this version to “Tape shelf” at any score. It becomes the disabled “Saved” button afterward. Saving a qualifying standard take also approves the order.
- “Choose for air” appears after saving and selects the take as that sport’s broadcast entry. The selected state reads “In program.”
- “Next order” appears on a saved take when the first unfinished order is a different clip. It opens that order on “Foley desk.”
- When multiple versions of the current clip are saved, “Take 1,” “Take 2,” and subsequent numbered controls select and play those versions.
- With no current take, the screen says “Record a take at the table to bring it to screening.” The “Foley desk” button returns to recording.

### “Timeline”

The editor shows “PUNCH-IN EDIT,” the clip title, and “EDIT MONITOR.” “Close timeline” is the accessibility label of the × button that returns to “Screening.” The timeline shows three waveforms, their cue marks, the selected range, and the playback cursor.

- “Contact,” “Movement,” and “Ambience” select the track to edit. The initial selection when opening an order is “Ambience.”
- “Range start” and “Range end” are the accessibility labels for the draggable range handles. Drag on a waveform to scrub the picture and move the playback cursor.
- “Solo” toggles playback of only the selected track. “Play from cursor” starts playback from the cursor. It changes to “Stop” during playback.
- “Re-record range” starts the countdown and opens “Prop table.” Only props for the highlighted track accept input. The rest of the mix plays as backing while the selected part is re-recorded. “Cancel” discards the punch-in. At the end of the range, the revised take opens in “Screening.”
- The on-screen instruction reads “Drag the handles to set a range. Scrub the waveform to move the cursor. Re-record the highlighted track.”

### Full-screen playback

The clip opens on a full-screen “PLAYBACK MONITOR” with the clip title and without the tab bar. “Play this take” restarts the current take. “Play first take” restarts the first version when it is available for comparison. “Stop” appears during playback. “Close full screen” is the accessibility label of the × button that returns to “Screening.”

### “Tape shelf”

“YOUR TAPES” and “Tape shelf” head four sport sections. Each starts with “No saved takes.” Saved versions appear as numbered cassette cards with a clip title and preview image. Tapping a cassette opens that exact saved version in “Screening.”

- “Choose for air” selects a tape for its sport. The selected tape says “In program.” Choosing another tape for that sport replaces the selection, while both tapes stay on the shelf.
- Before the broadcast is available, the shelf shows instructions with the current count, starting with “Choose a tape for each sport (0 / 4) and finish every order to go on air.”
- “Final broadcast” appears once all 16 orders are approved and one tape from each sport is selected. It starts playback immediately.

### “On the air”

The monitor shows “ON AIR,” “On the air,” and a position and clip title such as “1 / 4 · Into the net.” The four chosen takes play in order from “Soccer” through “Tennis,” “Basketball,” and “Track cycling.”

- “Stop” ends playback. “Replay program” appears after stopping or finishing and restarts from the first tape.
- “Open this order” opens the order for the clip currently shown on “Foley desk.”
- “Tape shelf” returns to the cassette collection and stops the broadcast.

### Save alert

A failed save, order selection, or broadcast selection can show the alert “Sporting Beat” with “The studio file couldn’t be saved. Try again.” “OK” dismisses the alert so the player can retry.

## Features

- Sixteen “Director’s orders” across “Soccer,” “Tennis,” “Basketball,” and “Track cycling.”
- Looping “PICTURE ONLY” rehearsal with coloured cues and an auditionable “Prop table.”
- Tap, swipe, and sustained prop sounds with touch-controlled intensity. Sounds can overlap across “Contact,” “Movement,” and “Ambience.”
- “Record” with a three-second countdown, cue scoring, and per-cue timing, sound, and release feedback.
- “Creative take” for making and saving a soundtrack with the cue guides hidden.
- “Timeline” with waveforms, scrubbing, “Solo,” and “Re-record range” for one track over a chosen interval.
- “Screening” with first-versus-current playback and “Full screen.”
- “Tape shelf” with saved versions and one “Choose for air” selection per sport.
- “Final broadcast” of the four selected takes in sequence on the device.

## Behaviours that can look like bugs

- **Locked orders.** Orders unlock in sequence across all four sports. Meet the preceding order’s cue target and special conditions in a standard take, then tap “Save take.” Seeing “Order approved” or “Precision cut” before saving is only the review result.
- **An audible sound can miss a cue.** Use the correct type of prop on the corresponding track within “±350 ms.” Sustained cues also check when the sound is released. “Precise” uses a tighter 150 ms window. Use the cue rows in “Screening” to fix timing, sound choice, or release.
- **Special conditions can hold back approval.** “Drop shot” shows “Special condition · silence 00:04.00–00:05.85.” Keep the interval free of sound, including tails from earlier actions. “Off the rim,” “At the buzzer,” and all four cycling orders show “Special condition · land a cue on all 3 tracks.” The last three cycling orders also show “Drag while holding rubber to build intensity.” Match the “Slipstream” cue and increase the sound while holding “Stretched rubber.”
- **Preview loops and prop auditions are temporary.** “PICTURE ONLY” repeats while rehearsing. Tap “Record” to capture prop actions. Props wait through “STAND BY.” When the recording or punch-in reaches its end, the app automatically goes to “Screening.”
- **Recording locks the editor.** During a punch-in, “Re-record range” is disabled. “Solo,” the track buttons, the range handles, scrubbing, playback, and “Close timeline” wait until capture ends or is cancelled. Props outside the selected track are dimmed. The range handles also preserve at least 0.4 seconds between them. On “Into the net,” the editor initially selects “00:03.90” to “00:06.15.”
- **Creative work saves without advancing the campaign.** “Creative take” can be saved and chosen for air. To approve an order, switch “Creative take” off, record and meet the brief, and tap “Save take.”
- **Saving grows the version list.** A saved version’s button reads “Saved” and is disabled. Make or edit another take to save another version. “Play first take” requires a separate first take to compare, and numbered take selectors appear after more than one version of that clip is saved.
- **“Next order” is conditional.** It appears only on a saved take when another clip is the first unfinished order. If the current order still needs approval, revise it or return to “Orders.” If every order is approved, use “Tape shelf” for the broadcast.
- **The collection begins empty.** “Record a take at the table to bring it to screening.” clears when a take is recorded. “No saved takes” clears separately for each sport after its first save. When the shelf says “Choose a tape for each sport (0 / 4) and finish every order to go on air.”, save and select a tape for each sport and finish approving all 16 orders.
- **Leaving a screen or interrupting the app can stop playback or recording.** Return to “Foley desk” to restart its rehearsal loop or start a fresh recording. “Replay program” always starts the broadcast again from the first tape.

## Starter content and resume

The app includes 16 animated sports orders, 10 playable props with built-in sounds, and cue guides and briefs. It starts with no saved takes, approved orders, or broadcast selections.

Saved takes, order approvals, the last opened or saved order, and broadcast selections survive a relaunch. A returning player starts in “Orders” and can reopen saved work for playback or further editing. An in-progress recording, an unsaved take or edit, the editor’s range and cursor, and the broadcast playback position do not resume after a cold launch.

## Permissions

None. “Record” captures actions on the built-in props. The app has no microphone prompt or other permission usage description.

## Absent

- Login or accounts
- In-app purchases
- Ads
- Analytics
- Account deletion flow
- App Tracking Transparency prompt

Player-created soundtrack takes are present as local user-generated content. There is no public posting, sharing, or upload control.

## Data and support

Takes, approvals, the last order, and broadcast selections stay in the app on the device. “Final broadcast” plays the selected clips locally. There is no settings screen, on-screen “Contact” or “Support” control, or user-facing export or sync control.

## Platform

- Native iPhone and iPad app. Minimum version is iOS 17.0 or iPadOS 17.0.
- iPhone supports portrait and both landscape orientations. iPad also supports upside-down portrait. Wide layouts can put the clip and its controls side by side. Full-screen playback works within the supported orientations.
- The app is localized in US English, UK English, Arabic, and Traditional Chinese, with UK English as the development language. Gameplay and order availability follow the same rules across regions. Arabic uses right-to-left interface layout while the timeline and monitor progress advance left to right.
- In UK English, “Soccer” appears as “Football,” “Penalty kick in the rain” as “Penalty in the rain,” “Final straightaway” as “Home straight,” “Pillow” as “Cushion,” and “Corrugated cardboard” as “Corrugated card.” The opening brief reads “Cushion at the kick. Keys at the net. Card when the crowd stands.” The broadcast controls read “In programme” and “Replay programme.”
